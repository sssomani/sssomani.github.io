About

I’m a physician-engineer and cardiovascular medicine fellow at Stanford University. My research focuses on using cardiovascular digital signals, including electrocardiograms and intracardiac recordings, to improve arrhythmia detection and management. I’m particularly interested in cardiac electrophysiology and machine learning methods that can inform clinical care.

I studied chemical and biomolecular engineering at Georgia Tech and received my MD from the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai. During medical school, I co-founded Monogram Orthopedics, where I developed software for patient-specific implants and robotic surgical planning. I subsequently completed internal medicine residency and served as Chief Resident at Stanford.

Alongside my work on cardiac signals, I use language models to study public perceptions of cardiovascular disease and understand gaps in guideline-recommended care.
